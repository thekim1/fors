Gateway benchmark · 19 Nov 2026

Bifrost vs. the Ume LLM gateway

Both gateways ran on the same machine, in the same session, against the same simulated model, with their governance features on: virtual keys, rate limits, budgets at three levels, routing with a fallback, and a record of every message. On the same four vCPUs the Ume gateway served 4–7× as many messages at 5–7× less CPU per message, and it recorded all of them. Bifrost dropped up to 39 % of its request logs under load.

As load grows

Streamed chat on 4 vCPUs each, 20 s per point, an instant fake model, so everything measured is the gateway's own cost. Neither gateway returned a single error. Bifrost is at its limit from 8 clients: its throughput stays flat and the extra clients queue. The Ume gateway keeps gaining up to 256.

Lost request logs

Bifrost queues each request log for a background writer and drops the entry when the 10 000-entry queue is full, without slowing the request. Its own counter, 30 s non-streamed:

A small host

The Ume gateway's small-host budget for both: 0.5 vCPU and 512 MB, Postgres with 1 vCPU and 1 GB, realistic streams, mixed traffic at fixed rates for 2 minutes each. The Ume gateway also ran PII detection and the content guard; Bifrost's open-source build has neither.

Caveats. One session on one laptop (Intel i7-13700H, WSL2, CPU-pinned containers), single 20–30 s samples. The hybrid CPU makes the absolute figures lower than the Ume gateway's 2026-11-02 baseline (68–83 %), so compare the ratios. The fake model answers instantly except in the realistic-timing and small-host tests; with a real model neither gateway is noticeable to users, and the difference is the hardware they need.
